# 04 — Go-to-Market: Dubai Launch & City Expansion

## Why Dubai first
- **Density and mix:** a very high concentration of regional HQs, family groups, free-zone companies and fast-growing founders. Many are newcomers who actively want peers.
- **Event culture and sponsor budgets:** banks, wealth managers, law firms and free zones already sponsor executive events. We redirect existing budgets rather than inventing new ones.
- **Gateway:** Dubai members pull in Abu Dhabi, Riyadh and Doha peers naturally. Expansion follows real relationships.
- **Ecosystem support:** accelerators and free-zone programmes (in5, DIFC, Dubai Chamber, DMCC) help with credibility and introductions.

## Launch sequence (Dubai)

### Phase 0: concierge MVP (months 0–2), before the app exists
- Hand-pick **50 Founding Members** across about 15 sectors, using the founders' own networks plus 2–3 well-connected "super-connector" CEOs as co-founders or advisors.
- Run the product **manually**: a weekly intro email or WhatsApp message with 3 matches, done by hand by the founding team, plus one dinner a month.
- This validates matching criteria, intro acceptance rates and sponsor appetite. It also yields testimonials and a sponsor pipeline **before writing serious code**.
- Sign **the first 1–2 Founding Partners**, for example a private bank and a law firm, on a discounted "founding" rate in exchange for case-study rights.

### Phase 1: private beta (months 3–5)
- App in TestFlight and Play internal testing, used by about 100 founding members.
- **Founding Member badge** and lifetime perks, such as priority event seats and the right to host a Circle.
- 4 events, 8 seeded Circles, weekly intros running in the app.

### Phase 2: invite-only public launch (month 6)
- **Launch moment:** a flagship "Flowly Dubai 100" evening with about 100 CEOs. Partner-hosted, with press under embargo (Gulf News, The National, Arabian Business, Forbes Middle East).
- **Waitlist plus invitations:** public waitlist with "jump the line" through a member invitation. Scarcity is real because every applicant is reviewed.
- **Content engine:** "Dubai CEO Pulse #1" report, short video interviews with Founding Members, and the founder posting on LinkedIn. We use LinkedIn as a **distribution** channel even though the product is a deliberate alternative to it.
- **Target:** 300 members by month 9 and 600 by month 12.

### Acquisition channels, ranked
1. **Member invitations** (3 per member per quarter): highest quality, lowest cost.
2. **Events:** every event includes 3–5 vetted non-member guests who get fast-track applications.
3. **Partner client introductions:** banks and law firms invite their CEO clients, which is a value-add for them.
4. **Ecosystem partners:** free-zone welcome packs (DMCC, DIFC, DSO, Dubai Chamber), accelerators and VC portfolios.
5. **PR and thought leadership:** CEO Pulse reports and founder visibility.
6. **Targeted LinkedIn ads** to verified job-title audiences (CEO/MD in UAE), used sparingly and mainly for waitlist fill.

## Partner sales motion
- **Ideal first partners:** private banks and wealth managers, international law firms, Big 4 and boutique consultancies, free zones, premium auto and aviation brands, family-office service providers.
- **Offer ladder:** (1) single event sponsorship → (2) quarterly series → (3) annual City Partner.
- **Proof pack:** member profile mix (anonymised sector and stage breakdown), event photos, attendee satisfaction score, follow-up meeting rate.
- **Sales team:** one Partnerships Lead from month 2, with a target of 2 City Partners plus 6 event sponsors in year 1.

## City Launch Kit (the repeatable expansion playbook)

Each new city follows the same steps:

| Step | Deliverable | Timing |
|---|---|---|
| 1. City thesis | Size the CEO population, sector mix, sponsor landscape, regulatory check (data law, events permits, company presence) | T−4 months |
| 2. City Host | Hire or appoint a **City Lead**: a well-networked local operator, usually ex-banking, ex-consulting or community | T−3 months |
| 3. Founding 50 | Recruit 50 local Founding Members, using existing members' introductions to that city | T−3 → T−1 |
| 4. Anchor partners | Sign at least 2 City Partners (ideally regional extensions of existing partners) plus 1 government or economic-development partner | T−2 months |
| 5. Local compliance | Data-transfer basis, local entity or branch if needed, events licensing, Arabic and local-language content | T−2 months |
| 6. Launch evening | City "100" evening, press, waitlist opens | T |
| 7. Density milestones | 150 members by T+3 months and 300 by T+6. If missed, pause and fix before opening the next city | T+6 months |

**Go / no-go rule for each next city:** the previous city must reach **≥ 40% of members active each month**, **≥ 2 renewed partners**, and **positive event margin**.

## Expansion order

| Wave | City | Why | Indicative launch |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Dubai** | Home base | Month 6 |
| 2 | **Abu Dhabi** | 90 minutes away; capital, sovereign funds, ADGM. Shares partners and members with Dubai | Month 12 |
| 2 | **Riyadh** | Largest GCC economy, Vision 2030 growth, many Dubai CEOs already expanding into KSA. Needs a local City Lead and KSA PDPL compliance | Month 15 |
| 3 | **Doha** | Strong family groups and investment funds; GCC continuity | Month 20 |
| 3 | **London** | The UAE–UK corridor, many Gulf CEOs keep a London base. First non-GCC test (UK GDPR) | Month 24 |
| 3 | **Singapore** | Asia hub; mirrors Dubai's international HQ profile | Month 28 |
| 4 | Mumbai, Cairo, Istanbul, New York | Corridor cities with heavy Dubai traffic | Year 3+ |

**City Passport** (see [02](02-features-and-ux.md)) turns every new city into value for existing members, because a Dubai CEO visiting Riyadh can meet local members. That is the network effect that makes each new city easier to launch than the last.
